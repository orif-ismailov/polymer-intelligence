## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Structured Data Extraction (single-call, fixed-schema classify + extract)

**Description:**
A userbot (MTProto) monitors 10–20 agreed public Telegram channels and writes new messages to `raw_items`. Each message gets exactly one LLM call that (a) classifies relevant/not and (b) on relevance extracts a fixed-schema polymer market signal — type, product, grade, volume, price, currency, counterparty, urgency — as strict JSON validated with Pydantic. Output, model id, prompt version, token counts and latency are journaled in `parse_runs`; signals/requests get a lead_score (0–1, HOT/MEDIUM/LOW) from rules + LLM. Confidence <0.5 routes the signal to a `needs_review` queue surfaced in the dashboard. Users are the internal analyst/trader team; "good" means accurate, auditable, budget-bounded signals in the same unified feed, with low-confidence items safely escalated rather than silently wrong. Acceptance (TZ §6.1.3): on the customer's 100-message control sample, relevant-signal recall ≥80% and field precision on detected signals ≥85%.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
<!-- The 3-5 behaviors that absolutely cannot go wrong in this system -->
1. **Fabricated fields** — asserting a value (price/volume/grade/counterparty) the source message does not contain. A hallucinated price feeds the feed/alerts and misleads trading decisions. Missing fields MUST be `null`, never invented.
2. **Silent schema/validation failure → partial or dropped signal** — a malformed LLM response must mark the `parse_run` failed and dead-letter for review, never emit a partial/invented record or crash the Celery worker.
3. **False-negative relevance** — discarding a genuine market signal as "not relevant" (drops recall below the ≥80% gate; the team never sees the lead).
4. **Budget/cost runaway or hard stop** — exceeding the daily token budget must degrade to rule-based fallback + nightly catch-up with an admin alert, never silently burn spend or block the pipeline indefinitely.
5. **Unattributable output** — a signal whose `parse_run` lacks model id + prompt_version + tokens, breaking the audit trail and making eval-replay/regression impossible.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** B2B polymer/petrochemical commodity trading — Uzbekistan domestic market (UZEX exchange + Telegram channel monitoring). Products: PP, HDPE, LDPE, LLDPE, PVC, PET, PS, ABS. Prices quoted in USD/MT and UZS/MT; CBU official FX rates apply. Primary exchange venue: UZEX (Tashkent). Language surface: Russian + Uzbek + transliterated Latin Uzbek.

**User Population:** Internal analysts and traders (roles: analyst, trader) at a Tashkent-based polymer trading company. Users act directly on extracted signals — they phone or message counterparties, adjust bid/ask positions, or escalate HOT leads to management. They do not re-read raw Telegram messages; they trust the extracted record in the dashboard feed.

**Stakes Level:** High

**Output Consequence:** A signal in the feed is treated as an actionable lead. An analyst who sees "PP raffia, 50 MT, $850/MT, EXW Tashkent, seller: Shurtan GCC" will contact the counterparty and begin a price negotiation. A fabricated price, a wrong product type, a bid/ask reversal, or a stale repost counted as a new offer all feed bad information into real commercial decisions — wasted calls, mispriced offers, and missed deals. A false-negative (missed relevant signal) is also a direct cost: the team never learns about a live buy/sell opportunity.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

---

**Dimension:** Product and grade disambiguation

Good (trader accepts): Extracted `product` is the correct polymer code (e.g., `PP`) and `grade_text` faithfully reproduces the grade or application descriptor from the source — e.g., "T30S", "PP raffia", "H030 SG", "injection grade". Trade names are preserved verbatim; the product code is normalized to the DB enum (`PP`, `HDPE`, etc.).

Bad (trader flags): "PP film" extracted as `product: HDPE`; grade "T30S" mapped to the wrong polymer; a Shurtan trade name ("Navruz") output as the grade rather than the product; grade field left null when the message clearly states a grade; or a generic product code assigned when the message specifies a sub-type (e.g., PP raffia vs. PP injection vs. PP fiber).

Stakes: Critical

Source: Polymer grade disambiguation is the single most common source of mismatched deals in Central Asian polymer trading. Wrong grade means a buyer receives off-spec material. Product-grade code standards: Shurtan GCC (Uzbekistan), Sibur/Gazprom (Russia), Sinopec (China) each use distinct trade names that map to the same polymer types. Customer-provided synonyms/channel lists are the authoritative mapping.

---

**Dimension:** Signal kind — buy request vs. sell offer vs. deal vs. price quote

Good (trader accepts): `kind` matches the communicative intent of the message. "Куплю ПП рафия 50 тонн" → `buy_request`. "Продаём HDPE 2420D, 100 MT, $920" → `sell_offer`. "Сделка закрыта по $870" → `deal`. "Цена на ПВХ сегодня 1 150 000 сум/кг" → `price_quote`.

Bad (trader flags): A seller's offer classified as `buy_request` (causes the analyst to look for a supplier instead of a buyer, or to contact a potential seller as if they are a buyer); a completed deal reported as a live offer; a price list misclassified as `sell_offer` with a specific counterparty and volume when those are absent.

Stakes: Critical

Source: Bid/ask reversal is a well-documented failure mode in B2B commodity intelligence systems (LiveTradeBench, arxiv 2511.03628; commodity AI system design, Medium 2026). In polymer trading, contacting the wrong side of a transaction causes reputational damage and wasted commercial effort.

---

**Dimension:** Price and currency correctness

Good (trader accepts): `price` is extracted as a numeric value matching what appears in the source. `currency` is the correct ISO code: USD when the source says "доллар"/"$"/"у.е.", UZS when the source says "сум"/"so'm", RUB when the source says "рублей"/"₽". Price range ("850–900 $/MT") is handled — either the midpoint or the lower bound is extracted with a null or range note; the field is never invented when absent.

Bad (trader flags): UZS price (e.g., 9,500,000 UZS/MT) recorded as USD 9,500,000 (a ~10,000× error); price extracted as a round number when the source gives a range and no range is signalled; currency defaulted to USD when the source is ambiguous but context implies UZS; "1 150 тыс. сум/кг" converted to MT by the LLM without flagging the unit conversion.

Stakes: Critical

Source: Currency disambiguation between USD, UZS, and RUB is a persistent practical error in Central Asian market data work (practitioner knowledge; CBU rate import in DB schema confirms the multi-currency reality). UZS/USD confusion produces order-of-magnitude pricing errors. Prices in this market: PP ~$850–1,050/MT (USD); HDPE ~$900–1,100/MT; PVC ~$900–1,200/MT; PET ~$800–950/MT (as of 2026 regional benchmarks).

---

**Dimension:** Volume and unit normalization

Good (trader accepts): `volume` is in metric tonnes (MT) and `volume_unit` is `MT`. Source values like "50 тонн", "50 000 кг", "50 т" are all correctly normalized to 50 MT. If the source states kg, the extraction converts to MT and records the original. If volume is absent, `volume` is null — never defaulted to 1 or 0.

Bad (trader flags): "50 000 кг" extracted as `volume: 50000` with `volume_unit: MT` (50× error); "5 вагонов" left as-is without a null flag and without noting the unit ambiguity; volume present in source but null in output; volume invented where source says only "небольшая партия" (small batch).

Stakes: High

Source: Volume unit errors (kg vs. MT) are a documented LLM structured-extraction failure mode (relational binding drift, arxiv 2602.10881). In polymer trading, a 50× volume error directly misprices a deal.

---

**Dimension:** Stale/forwarded repost vs. new market event

Good (trader accepts): A forwarded message or a repost of an earlier offer (e.g., "Forwarded from @polymer_uz_market" with a date 3 days ago) is either (a) classified `is_relevant: false` with a note that it is a repost with no new price/volume data, or (b) extracted with `event_at` set to the original post date, not the forward date — clearly flagged so the analyst knows this is not a fresh offer.

Bad (trader flags): A 3-day-old offer reposted today is extracted as a new `sell_offer` with today's timestamp, causing the analyst to contact a counterparty about an offer that has already been filled or withdrawn.

Stakes: High

Source: Stale-repost misclassification is a well-known operational problem in Telegram-based market monitoring (practitioner knowledge; Telegram's forwarded-message header includes original channel and date, which Telethon exposes via `fwd_from`). This is specific to the Telegram-as-marketplace context, not generic hallucination.

---

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

1. **Grade/trade-name synonym collapse.** Russian and Uzbek polymer channels mix IUPAC names, producer trade names, application descriptors, and transliterated Latin variants for the same product. "Naruz", "T30S", "рафия", "PP-R" can all refer to polypropylene but to different grades with different prices. The LLM must not collapse these into a single `grade_text`; it must preserve the verbatim source text and not invent a canonical grade name. Failure rate is highest on Russian-language Uzbek-specific trade names not in the model's training data.

2. **Bid/ask reversal on ambiguous channel posts.** Telegram posts in CIS polymer channels frequently omit explicit "buy" or "sell" verbs — price and grade are stated without a speech act marker. Without clear "куплю"/"продам" signals, the LLM may default to the more common `sell_offer` even for buy requests. This is especially common for posts from traders (as opposed to producers), who post both sides. A misclassified `kind` triggers the wrong alert rules and sends analysts toward the wrong action.

3. **Currency and price confusion on UZS-denominated posts.** UZS prices for polymers are large integers (1,000,000–15,000,000 UZS/MT range). Posts from local Uzbek sellers frequently quote in UZS without an explicit "сум" or "so'm" label — the number alone. The LLM may default to USD, producing a price 12,000× off. Additionally, "у.е." (conventional units) in CIS trade language almost always means USD but is not an ISO code; the LLM must map "у.е." → `USD`.

4. **Spam, ad, and non-market chatter classified as relevant.** Telegram channels for polymer trading carry a significant volume of channel self-promotion, off-topic logistics posts, equipment sales, job offers, and political commentary alongside genuine market signals. An LLM that is calibrated to maximize recall will over-classify these as relevant. The effect is feed pollution — analysts receive low-quality leads and lose trust in the system faster than from missed signals.

5. **Repost of stale price counted as a new signal.** Telegram forwarding mechanics mean a 1-week-old price quote can arrive in monitored channels at any time. Telethon exposes `fwd_from.date` on forwarded messages. If the extractor ignores `fwd_from.date` and uses `fetched_at` as `event_at`, the signal appears fresh when it is not. Forwarded messages from news aggregators (not original market participants) should be classified with lower confidence or flagged for human review.

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

**Telegram ToS / userbot account risk:** Monitoring public channels via a userbot (MTProto/Telethon) is technically compliant with Telegram's terms for public channels but carries account-restriction risk if the account triggers rate limits or is flagged for automated activity. This risk is customer-borne (per PROJECT.md: "Userbot account-restriction risk borne by customer"). The system must implement polite fetch rates and respect Telegram's flood-wait errors (`FloodWaitError`) to minimize this risk.

**Personal data / counterparty PII:** Channel posts may contain personal phone numbers, Telegram usernames, or company names of counterparties. The `counterparty_text` field stores this verbatim. Staff access to this data is controlled by RBAC (analyst/trader roles per `staff_role` ENUM). The deployment is internal (no public API); data does not leave the VPS. No GDPR or regulatory data-residency constraint applies to this Uzbekistan-internal deployment, but the team should not republish raw counterparty data externally without consent.

**No financial advice regulation:** This system is explicitly scoped as a market-intelligence and observation tool (TZ §1.1: "system explains observed factors only; AI is advisory; human makes the final decision"). No financial advisory regulation (FCA, SEC, etc.) applies. The AI output carries no legal weight as investment or trading advice. The practical consequence is that accuracy stakes are operational (bad decisions, missed deals) rather than regulatory (fines, license risk).

**UZEX data attribution:** UZEX-sourced signals require mandatory attribution "По данным uzex.uz" in published content (PROJECT.md). This applies to reports and public-channel outputs, not to the internal extraction pipeline.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** Anthropic Python SDK (`anthropic`) + `instructor` + Pydantic 2 — direct SDK, no orchestration framework

**Version:** Pin in `backend/pyproject.toml` to the same discipline as ruff/mypy (e.g. `anthropic>=0.40,<1.0`, `instructor>=1.5,<2.0`); pin exact at lockfile. Add a mypy override if `instructor` ships without `py.typed`. Model: latest Claude (configurable via `DEC-llm-models`; default the cost-efficient tier, e.g. Claude Haiku 4.5, for per-message extraction).

**Rationale:**
The workload is a single LLM call returning a fixed Pydantic schema — the canonical "direct SDK over framework" case (ai-frameworks Anti-Patterns #1 and #5). An orchestration framework (LangChain/LlamaIndex/CrewAI/LangGraph) would add abstraction overhead, p99 latency risk, and a large dependency surface for zero benefit on one-shot extraction. `instructor` wraps the native Anthropic client to validate/retry directly into the Pydantic 2 models the codebase already uses everywhere, so the extractor is a thin, fully-typed service that runs inside existing Celery `parse`-queue tasks, persists to Postgres (`parse_runs`, `signals.ai`), and logs via structlog — zero new infrastructure, no model-orchestration layer to maintain.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| LangChain (`with_structured_output`) | Runner-up. Abstraction tax + larger dep surface for a one-shot call. Switch ONLY if extraction grows into a multi-step retrieval/branching/tool-calling chain, or provider-swap-behind-one-interface becomes a hard requirement before Phase 6. |
| LangGraph | Graph/state machinery is overkill for a stateless single call. |
| LlamaIndex | RAG/retrieval-first; no retrieval in this extraction path. |
| CrewAI / AutoGen | Multi-agent orchestration — wrong shape entirely. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** Partial — Anthropic (Claude) at the model layer is accepted, isolated behind a single `extractor` service interface so the swap cost stays low. `instructor` is provider-agnostic, easing a future move.

**Eval & Observability decisions (carried into Sections 5–7):**
- **Eval tooling:** code-based **pytest** harness — golden set sourced from Postgres/CSV, per-field recall/precision assertions, runs in existing pytest infra/CI. No external eval platform for Phase 5.
- **Tracing:** **DB journal sufficient** — `parse_runs` (prompt_version, model id, tokens, latency, raw response, validation outcome) + structlog. No external tracing service (Langfuse/Phoenix/LangSmith) this phase.

**Selection-time risks for the planner to carry forward:**
1. **`instructor` JSON-reask vs. Claude native tool-use** — two different schema-adherence/retry profiles. Spike both against 10–20 golden rows early in Phase 5 and pin the winner; it dictates retry semantics and the validation-failure metric.
2. **Prompt-version pinning is mandatory** — since tracing is DB-journal-only, the `parse_runs` row is the sole replay artifact. Store `prompt_version` (hash/semver) + `model_id` on every run; gate golden-set assertions on a known prompt version.
3. **Define degradation, not crashes** — bounded retries on schema-validation failure → mark `parse_run` failed with raw response captured → dead-letter for human review; token-budget overrun → rule-based fallback + nightly catch-up + admin alert; API error/timeout inside Celery → retry/fail cleanly. Never silently emit a partial/invented record.

### Entry Point Pattern

Two transport options are available; both are spike candidates (see Implementation Guidance).

**Option A — instructor TOOLS mode (recommended spike default)**
```python
import anthropic
import instructor
from instructor.exceptions import InstructorRetryException
from pydantic import BaseModel

# Patch once at module level (not inside the Celery task)
_raw_client = anthropic.Anthropic()  # reads ANTHROPIC_API_KEY from env
client = instructor.from_anthropic(_raw_client, mode=instructor.Mode.TOOLS)

class MySchema(BaseModel):
    value: str

def extract(message_text: str) -> tuple[MySchema, anthropic.types.Message]:
    """Returns (validated_result, raw_completion) for token journaling."""
    result, completion = client.messages.create_with_completion(
        model="claude-haiku-4-5",   # configurable; see Section 4
        max_tokens=512,
        temperature=0,
        system=[
            {
                "type": "text",
                "text": SYSTEM_PROMPT,          # loaded from parsing/prompts/extract_vN.md
                "cache_control": {"type": "ephemeral"},   # cache the large schema prompt
            }
        ],
        messages=[{"role": "user", "content": message_text}],
        response_model=MySchema,
        max_retries=2,              # instructor retries on ValidationError
    )
    # completion.usage carries input_tokens, output_tokens, cache_read_input_tokens
    return result, completion
```

### Key Abstractions

| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| `instructor.from_anthropic(client, mode=Mode.TOOLS)` | Patches the raw Anthropic client to accept `response_model`; handles JSON reask loop internally | Module-level singleton; never patch inside a Celery task body |
| `create_with_completion(response_model=...)` | Returns `(validated_pydantic_obj, raw_Message)` so you get both the schema AND usage tokens | Every extraction call; raw completion is required for token journaling |
| `max_retries=N` on `create`/`create_with_completion` | Instructor automatically retries with the validation error appended to the conversation | Set to 2; on `InstructorRetryException` mark parse_run failed and dead-letter |
| `cache_control: {"type": "ephemeral"}` on system block | Caches the static system prompt + schema description for 5 min; cache reads cost 0.1× input price | Apply to the system prompt block; never to the per-message user block |
| `Mode.TOOLS` vs `Mode.JSON` | TOOLS uses Claude's native function-calling for schema adherence; JSON uses text extraction + regex | TOOLS is more reliable on complex schemas; JSON is a fallback if tool-use quota issues arise |

### Common Pitfalls

1. **Patching the client inside the Celery task function** — `instructor.from_anthropic()` is not free; it creates wrapper objects. Patch once at module import time and reuse. Creating a new patched client per task wastes time and risks connection pool exhaustion.

2. **Not using `create_with_completion`** — `client.messages.create(response_model=...)` returns only the Pydantic object; you lose `completion.usage` and cannot journal `tokens_in`/`tokens_out` to `parse_runs`. Always call `create_with_completion`.

3. **Placing `cache_control` on the user message instead of the system block** — The user message changes per Telegram message; a cache breakpoint there will never hit. The cache breakpoint must be on the static system prompt block. Claude Haiku 4.5 requires ≥4,096 tokens to activate caching; ensure the system prompt + schema description clears that threshold (add few-shot examples to pad if necessary).

4. **Leaving `temperature` unset or non-zero for extraction** — Extraction is deterministic by intent. Omitting `temperature` uses the model default (which may not be 0). Always set `temperature=0` explicitly; without it, identical inputs produce variant field values and break golden-set regression.

5. **Catching `ValidationError` instead of `InstructorRetryException`** — After `max_retries` are exhausted, instructor raises `InstructorRetryException`, not `pydantic.ValidationError`. Catching only `ValidationError` silently misses the dead-letter path and may emit a partial result.

6. **Emitting a partial/invented record on validation failure** — If `InstructorRetryException` is raised, the LLM could not produce a schema-valid response. The correct action is: persist the raw response to `parse_runs.result`, set `parse_runs.status = 'error'`, mark `raw_items.parse_status = 'failed'`, and enqueue for human review. Never write a partially-validated signal to `signals`.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model Configuration:**

| Parameter | Value | Reason |
|-----------|-------|--------|
| `model` | `claude-haiku-4-5` (default; configurable via `DEC-llm-models` setting) | Cost-efficient tier; ~10× cheaper than Sonnet for equivalent extraction accuracy on structured fields |
| `temperature` | `0` | Extraction is deterministic — identical input must produce identical output for golden-set regression |
| `max_tokens` | `512` | Extraction output is a fixed JSON object; 512 is a comfortable ceiling; unbounded `max_tokens` in production is a cost anti-pattern |
| `max_retries` (instructor) | `2` | 1 retry is usually enough to fix a minor schema error; 3+ retries triples latency; anything past 2 retries should dead-letter |

**Transport spike — pin one approach early:**

Before writing production code, run a 10–20 row golden spike comparing both options:

| Option | Schema adherence | Retry semantics | Token overhead | Recommendation |
|--------|-----------------|-----------------|----------------|----------------|
| **instructor TOOLS mode** (`Mode.TOOLS`) | High — Claude's native tool-use forces structured output | instructor reasks with validation error in conversation history | ~50 extra tokens per retry (tool_use block) | Default spike candidate |
| **Claude native tool-use** (no instructor) | High — same underlying mechanism | Manual: catch `ValidationError`, reformat, resend | Same | Use if you want zero instructor dep |
| **instructor JSON mode** (`Mode.JSON`) | Medium — text extraction + regex; more brittle on optional fields | instructor reasks, but relies on text parsing | Slightly lower | Fallback only; avoid for ≥8-field schemas |

Pin the winner after the spike. The `parse_runs.parser` column records which path was used (`'llm_extract_tools'` vs `'llm_extract_json'`).

**Core Pattern:**

```python
# backend/parsing/extractor.py
import os
import anthropic
import instructor
from instructor.exceptions import InstructorRetryException
from .schemas import ExtractionResult, BudgetExceeded
from .budget import check_and_reserve_tokens
from .prompts.loader import load_prompt

# Module-level singleton — patched once, reused across all Celery tasks
_raw_client = anthropic.Anthropic(api_key=os.environ["ANTHROPIC_API_KEY"])
_client = instructor.from_anthropic(_raw_client, mode=instructor.Mode.TOOLS)

# Default model; overridden by DEC-llm-models config in production
DEFAULT_MODEL = os.environ.get("LLM_EXTRACTION_MODEL", "claude-haiku-4-5")
PROMPT_VERSION = os.environ.get("LLM_PROMPT_VERSION", "v1")  # pin in deploy config


def extract_signal(
    message_text: str,
    *,
    model: str = DEFAULT_MODEL,
    prompt_version: str = PROMPT_VERSION,
) -> tuple[ExtractionResult, dict]:
    """
    Classify and extract a polymer market signal from a Telegram message.

    Returns:
        (ExtractionResult, journal_data)  — journal_data is written to parse_runs.

    Raises:
        BudgetExceeded — caller must fall back to rule-based extraction.
        InstructorRetryException — all retries failed; caller must dead-letter.
    """
    # 1. Token-budget gate (Redis counter).  Raises BudgetExceeded if daily limit hit.
    check_and_reserve_tokens(estimated_tokens=400)  # conservative pre-check

    # 2. Load versioned prompt (immutable file; never edit existing versions).
    system_prompt = load_prompt(prompt_version)     # reads parsing/prompts/extract_v{N}.md

    # 3. LLM call with instructor validation.
    result, completion = _client.messages.create_with_completion(
        model=model,
        max_tokens=512,
        temperature=0,
        system=[
            {
                "type": "text",
                "text": system_prompt,
                # Cache the static system prompt.
                # Claude Haiku 4.5 requires >=4096 tokens to activate;
                # ensure the system prompt + schema + few-shot examples meet the threshold.
                "cache_control": {"type": "ephemeral"},
            }
        ],
        messages=[{"role": "user", "content": message_text}],
        response_model=ExtractionResult,
        max_retries=2,   # instructor retries on pydantic.ValidationError
    )

    # 4. Surface token usage for journaling.
    usage = completion.usage
    journal = {
        "model": model,
        "prompt_version": prompt_version,
        "tokens_in": usage.input_tokens + getattr(usage, "cache_creation_input_tokens", 0),
        "tokens_out": usage.output_tokens,
        "cache_read_tokens": getattr(usage, "cache_read_input_tokens", 0),
        "raw_response": completion.model_dump(),   # full completion stored for replay
    }

    return result, journal
```

```python
# backend/parsing/budget.py
import redis
from datetime import date

_redis = redis.from_url(os.environ["REDIS_URL"])
DAILY_TOKEN_LIMIT = int(os.environ.get("LLM_DAILY_TOKEN_LIMIT", 500_000))

class BudgetExceeded(Exception):
    """Raised when the daily token budget is exhausted.
    Caller must degrade to rule-based fallback and enqueue for nightly catch-up."""

def check_and_reserve_tokens(estimated_tokens: int) -> None:
    """Atomically check + increment the daily Redis counter.
    Key resets at UTC midnight (TTL = seconds until end of day).
    Raises BudgetExceeded if limit would be exceeded."""
    key = f"llm_tokens:{date.today().isoformat()}"
    pipe = _redis.pipeline()
    pipe.incrby(key, estimated_tokens)
    pipe.expireat(key, _seconds_until_midnight())
    new_total, _ = pipe.execute()
    if new_total > DAILY_TOKEN_LIMIT:
        # Roll back the reservation so the counter stays accurate.
        _redis.decrby(key, estimated_tokens)
        raise BudgetExceeded(f"Daily token budget {DAILY_TOKEN_LIMIT} exhausted")
```

```python
# backend/tasks/parse_tasks.py  (Celery task — orchestrates the full parse flow)
from celery import shared_task
from parsing.extractor import extract_signal
from parsing.schemas import BudgetExceeded, CONFIDENCE_REVIEW_THRESHOLD
from parsing.fallback import rule_based_extract
from instructor.exceptions import InstructorRetryException
import structlog

log = structlog.get_logger()

@shared_task(bind=True, max_retries=3, default_retry_delay=10)
def parse_raw_item(self, raw_item_id: int) -> None:
    raw = fetch_raw_item(raw_item_id)          # read from DB
    log_ctx = {"raw_item_id": raw_item_id}

    try:
        result, journal = extract_signal(raw.content)
        status = "ok"
        error_msg = None
    except BudgetExceeded:
        log.warning("budget_exceeded", **log_ctx)
        result = rule_based_extract(raw.content)  # degrade gracefully
        journal = {"model": None, "prompt_version": None,
                   "tokens_in": 0, "tokens_out": 0}
        status = "ok"                             # rule-based is valid output
        # Enqueue for nightly LLM catch-up:
        enqueue_nightly_reprocess(raw_item_id)
        error_msg = "budget_exceeded_fallback"
    except InstructorRetryException as exc:
        # All retries failed.  Dead-letter — never emit partial record.
        log.error("extraction_failed_all_retries", attempts=exc.n_attempts, **log_ctx)
        write_parse_run(raw_item_id, status="error",
                        error=str(exc), journal={"raw_response": str(exc.failed_attempts)})
        mark_raw_item_failed(raw_item_id)
        # Enqueue for human review:
        enqueue_needs_review(raw_item_id)
        return

    # Journal every run (mandatory for audit trail and eval replay).
    parse_run_id = write_parse_run(
        raw_item_id=raw_item_id,
        parser="llm_extract",
        model=journal["model"],
        prompt_version=journal["prompt_version"],
        tokens_in=journal["tokens_in"],
        tokens_out=journal["tokens_out"],
        result=result.model_dump() if result else None,
        status=status,
        error=error_msg,
    )

    if result and result.is_relevant:
        if result.confidence < CONFIDENCE_REVIEW_THRESHOLD:
            # Route to needs_review; write signal anyway so analysts can accept/reject.
            write_signal(result, needs_review=True)
        else:
            write_signal(result, needs_review=False)
        mark_raw_item_parsed(raw_item_id)
    else:
        mark_raw_item_irrelevant(raw_item_id)
```

```python
# backend/parsing/prompts/loader.py
from pathlib import Path
import functools

PROMPTS_DIR = Path(__file__).parent

@functools.lru_cache(maxsize=16)   # cache loaded prompt text in worker memory
def load_prompt(version: str) -> str:
    """Load parsing/prompts/extract_v{version}.md.
    Version string is stored verbatim in parse_runs.prompt_version.
    NEVER edit an existing prompt file — create a new version instead."""
    path = PROMPTS_DIR / f"extract_{version}.md"
    if not path.exists():
        raise FileNotFoundError(f"Prompt version {version!r} not found: {path}")
    return path.read_text(encoding="utf-8")
```

Prompt files are treated as immutable artifacts. When a prompt changes, create `extract_v2.md`; leave `extract_v1.md` untouched. This guarantees that any `parse_run` row can be replayed by re-running the extractor with `prompt_version` from that row.

**Tool Use:**

No external tools are called by the LLM. The only "tool" surface is the instructor `response_model` parameter, which maps to a Claude tool definition under the hood (`Mode.TOOLS`). This is intentional — the extraction call is a single synchronous classify+extract, not an agent loop.

Supporting integrations:
- **Redis** — token-budget counter (one key per UTC day, TTL = seconds until midnight)
- **Postgres** — `parse_runs` journal + `signals` write; use existing SQLAlchemy session from the Celery worker
- **Celery** — `parse` queue; the task is the orchestrator; no additional broker config needed beyond what Phase 4 established

**State Management:**

Extraction is stateless. All state lives in Postgres:
- `raw_items.parse_status` tracks processing state (`pending` → `parsed`/`failed`/`irrelevant`)
- `raw_items.parse_attempts` is incremented on each attempt (Celery task retry or manual requeue)
- `parse_runs` journals every LLM call independently — multiple `parse_runs` rows may exist for one `raw_item` (retries, prompt-version upgrades, manual reprocessing)
- `signals.ai` JSONB stores the latest AI enrichment (lead_score, urgency, model, prompt_version, scored_at); this column is the only mutable AI output — it is overwritten on re-scoring

The worker holds no in-process state between tasks. The instructor client and the loaded prompt are module-level singletons (safe for Celery fork/prefork).

**Context Window Strategy:**

Claude Haiku 4.5 has a 200K token context window. For single Telegram message extraction, context is never a concern — messages are typically 50–500 tokens.

The relevant concern is the system prompt size:
- The system prompt (schema description + instructions + few-shot examples) should be sized to clear the 4,096-token caching threshold for Claude Haiku 4.5. Below this threshold, every call pays full input price on the system prompt.
- Target: 4,200–6,000 tokens for the system prompt (schema JSON + 2–3 annotated few-shot examples). Measure with `anthropic.Anthropic().messages.count_tokens(...)` during prompt development.
- The user message (the Telegram message text) is always short and always comes after the cache breakpoint.
- Forwarded messages with quoted text can be 2–3× longer than originals. Truncate the user message to 2,000 tokens max before passing to the LLM; beyond that, the relevant signal is almost certainly in the first 1,000 tokens.

### 4b.1 Structured Outputs with Pydantic

**Schema definition — `backend/parsing/schemas.py`:**

```python
from __future__ import annotations

from decimal import Decimal
from enum import Enum
from typing import Optional
import re

from pydantic import BaseModel, Field, field_validator, model_validator


# --- Enumerations matching DB ENUMs exactly ---

class SignalKind(str, Enum):
    BUY_REQUEST = "buy_request"
    SELL_OFFER  = "sell_offer"
    DEAL        = "deal"
    PRICE_QUOTE = "price_quote"
    NEWS        = "news"


class UrgencyLevel(str, Enum):
    LOW    = "low"
    MEDIUM = "medium"
    HIGH   = "high"


# --- Field-level confidence (per-field) ---

class FieldConfidence(BaseModel):
    """Confidence score per extracted field (0.0–1.0).
    The LLM is instructed to populate this alongside each value.
    Used in eval: field-level precision is calculated per field."""
    product: float   = Field(ge=0.0, le=1.0, default=0.0)
    grade_text: float = Field(ge=0.0, le=1.0, default=0.0)
    volume: float    = Field(ge=0.0, le=1.0, default=0.0)
    price: float     = Field(ge=0.0, le=1.0, default=0.0)
    currency: float  = Field(ge=0.0, le=1.0, default=0.0)
    counterparty: float = Field(ge=0.0, le=1.0, default=0.0)
    region: float    = Field(ge=0.0, le=1.0, default=0.0)
    urgency: float   = Field(ge=0.0, le=1.0, default=0.0)


# --- Main extraction schema ---

class ExtractionResult(BaseModel):
    """
    Single-call extraction result for one Telegram message.

    Invariants enforced by validators:
    - If is_relevant=False, all market fields MUST be null.
    - volume and price are Decimal to avoid floating-point comparison bugs in eval.
    - currency is uppercased and validated as 3-char ISO 4217 code.
    - confidence is the overall score; <0.5 routes to needs_review queue.
    """

    # --- Relevance gate ---
    is_relevant: bool = Field(
        description="True if the message contains a polymer market signal."
    )

    # --- Signal classification (null when is_relevant=False) ---
    kind: Optional[SignalKind] = Field(
        default=None,
        description="Signal type matching signal_kind DB enum."
    )

    # --- Product fields ---
    product: Optional[str] = Field(
        default=None,
        max_length=32,
        description="Polymer code: PP, HDPE, LDPE, LLDPE, PVC, PET, PS, ABS, etc."
    )
    grade_text: Optional[str] = Field(
        default=None,
        max_length=128,
        description="Grade/specification verbatim from source. Null if not mentioned."
    )

    # --- Trade parameters ---
    volume: Optional[Decimal] = Field(
        default=None,
        ge=Decimal("0"),
        description="Volume in metric tonnes. Null if not stated."
    )
    volume_unit: str = Field(
        default="MT",
        description="Always MT for Phase 5."
    )
    price: Optional[Decimal] = Field(
        default=None,
        ge=Decimal("0"),
        description="Unit price. Null if not stated."
    )
    currency: Optional[str] = Field(
        default=None,
        min_length=3,
        max_length=3,
        description="ISO 4217 currency code: USD, UZS, RUB, CNY, EUR."
    )
    region: Optional[str] = Field(
        default=None,
        max_length=64,
        description="Market/region of the event. Null if not determinable."
    )

    # --- Counterparty ---
    counterparty_text: Optional[str] = Field(
        default=None,
        max_length=256,
        description="Counterparty name verbatim. Null if not mentioned."
    )

    # --- AI enrichment ---
    urgency: Optional[UrgencyLevel] = Field(
        default=None,
        description="Urgency level inferred from language cues."
    )
    lead_score: Optional[float] = Field(
        default=None,
        ge=0.0,
        le=1.0,
        description="Lead quality score 0–1. Populated by lead-scoring pass, not extraction."
    )

    # --- Confidence ---
    confidence: float = Field(
        ge=0.0,
        le=1.0,
        description="Overall extraction confidence. <0.5 routes to needs_review queue."
    )
    field_confidence: FieldConfidence = Field(
        default_factory=FieldConfidence,
        description="Per-field confidence scores."
    )

    # --- Validators ---

    @field_validator("currency")
    @classmethod
    def normalise_currency(cls, v: Optional[str]) -> Optional[str]:
        if v is None:
            return None
        upper = v.upper().strip()
        if not re.fullmatch(r"[A-Z]{3}", upper):
            raise ValueError(f"currency must be 3-letter ISO code, got {v!r}")
        return upper

    @model_validator(mode="after")
    def irrelevant_fields_must_be_null(self) -> "ExtractionResult":
        """If the message is not relevant, no market fields should be populated.
        This catches the hallucination pattern where the model invents values
        for an irrelevant message."""
        if not self.is_relevant:
            non_null = [
                f for f in ("kind", "product", "grade_text", "volume",
                            "price", "currency", "counterparty_text", "urgency")
                if getattr(self, f) is not None
            ]
            if non_null:
                raise ValueError(
                    f"is_relevant=False but market fields are populated: {non_null}. "
                    "Set all market fields to null for irrelevant messages."
                )
        return self


# --- Sentinel for token budget exhaustion ---

class BudgetExceeded(Exception):
    """Raised by budget.check_and_reserve_tokens() when daily limit is hit."""


# --- Review threshold constant (used in task orchestrator) ---
CONFIDENCE_REVIEW_THRESHOLD = 0.5
```

**How instructor integrates with this schema:**

`instructor` converts `ExtractionResult` to a Claude tool definition via `ExtractionResult.model_json_schema()` (Pydantic 2 native). When `Mode.TOOLS` is active, Claude returns a `tool_use` block whose `input` dict is then passed to `ExtractionResult.model_validate(input)`. If Pydantic raises `ValidationError`, instructor appends the error to the conversation and retries (up to `max_retries`).

**Retry logic:**

```
Attempt 1:  LLM call → ExtractionResult.model_validate() → ValidationError
            instructor appends: "Validation failed: <error details>. Fix and retry."
Attempt 2:  LLM call → validate → success  →  return result
Attempt 3:  (if attempt 2 also failed) → InstructorRetryException raised
```

- Log `InstructorRetryException.n_attempts` and `InstructorRetryException.failed_attempts` to structlog at `ERROR` level.
- Write the exception string to `parse_runs.error`; set `parse_runs.status = 'error'`.
- Enqueue `raw_item_id` to `needs_review` queue.
- Never write a partial signal to `signals`.

### 4b.2 Async-First Design

**This workload is Celery (sync), not async.** The Anthropic Python SDK supports both sync and async; use the sync interface inside Celery tasks.

The async interface (`anthropic.AsyncAnthropic`) is appropriate if you ever expose extraction as a FastAPI endpoint. The common mistake in that case is calling `asyncio.run()` inside an already-running event loop (e.g., inside a FastAPI route handler), which raises `RuntimeError: This event loop is already running`. Use `await` directly instead, or use `anyio.from_thread.run_sync()` to bridge sync code from an async context.

For Celery workers:
```python
# Correct: sync client in sync Celery task
_raw_client = anthropic.Anthropic()

@shared_task

```

If you later add a FastAPI streaming endpoint for live extraction preview:
```python
# Correct: async client in async FastAPI route — use create_partial for streaming
_async_client = instructor.from_anthropic(anthropic.AsyncAnthropic(), mode=instructor.Mode.TOOLS)

@app.post("/extraction/preview")
async def preview(text: str):

For Celery extraction tasks, always `await` (or block synchronously) — streaming returns `Partial[ExtractionResult]` objects that do not pass Pydantic validators until complete, so `model_validator` and `field_validator` decorators are skipped during streaming. Never use `create_partial` in the production extraction path.

### 4b.3 Prompt Engineering Discipline

**System vs. user prompt:**

The system prompt (loaded from `parsing/prompts/extract_v{N}.md`) contains:
1. Task description and output format specification
2. Full `ExtractionResult` JSON schema with field descriptions
3. 2–3 annotated few-shot examples (relevant and irrelevant)
4. Explicit null semantics: "If a field is not present in the source text, output `null`. Do not infer, estimate, or invent values."
5. The `cache_control` block is applied to this entire system block.

The user prompt contains only the raw Telegram message text — nothing else. No instructions, no schema reminders. Dynamic content must never appear before the cache breakpoint.

**Few-shot examples:** Inline in the system prompt (not dynamically retrieved). Three examples are optimal:
- One clear `sell_offer` with all fields present
- One `buy_request` with partial fields (volume present, price absent) to demonstrate null semantics
- One irrelevant message (forwarded news, emoji spam) showing `is_relevant: false` with all fields null

**`max_tokens`:** Always set explicitly. `512` is the ceiling for a fixed-schema extraction; the actual output is ~100–200 tokens. Leaving `max_tokens` unset in production risks a model producing verbose reasoning text instead of JSON when it is uncertain.

**Temperature:** Always `0` for extraction. Never omit it.

### 4b.4 Context Window Management

For single-message extraction, the context window is not a bottleneck. The concern is system prompt sizing for cache activation.

**Forwarded/edited Telegram messages:** Telethon delivers forwarded messages with the original message header prepended (`Forwarded from @channel_name\n\n`). Edited messages arrive as `UpdateEditMessage` events with the full new text. Both are handled the same way — truncate to 2,000 tokens before the LLM call:

```python
def prepare_message_text(raw: str, max_tokens: int = 2000) -> str:
    """Truncate long forwarded/media-caption messages before LLM call.
    Media captions are often empty; skip the call entirely if text is blank."""
    text = raw.strip()
    if not text:
        return ""   # caller should mark as irrelevant without LLM call
    # Rough token estimate: 1 token ≈ 4 chars for Russian/English mixed text
    if len(text) > max_tokens * 4:
        text = text[: max_tokens * 4] + "\n[TRUNCATED]"
    return text
```

**Media messages (photos, documents) with empty captions:** Skip the LLM call entirely; mark `parse_status = 'irrelevant'` immediately. The LLM cannot see images in this pipeline (no vision integration in Phase 5).

### 4b.5 Cost and Latency Budget

**Per-call cost estimate (Claude Haiku 4.5, as of 2026-06):**

| Token type | Count (estimate) | Price per 1M tokens | Cost per call |
|-----------|-----------------|---------------------|---------------|
| System prompt input (cache write, first call) | ~5,000 | $1.25 | $0.0063 |
| System prompt input (cache read, subsequent) | ~5,000 | $0.10 | $0.0005 |
| User message input (per message) | ~200 | $1.00 | $0.0002 |
| Output (extraction JSON) | ~150 | $5.00 | $0.0008 |
| **Steady-state per call (cache hit)** | | | **~$0.0015** |

At 1,000 messages/day: ~$1.50/day. At 10,000 messages/day: ~$15/day. The daily token budget (`LLM_DAILY_TOKEN_LIMIT` env var) is the hard ceiling; set it based on the approved monthly AI spend.

**Cache ROI:** Without prompt caching, the system prompt costs ~$0.005 per call (at 5,000 tokens × $1.00/1M). With caching active (cache hit rate expected >90% in steady state), that drops to ~$0.0005 — a 10× reduction on the largest cost component. This makes the 4,096-token threshold for Claude Haiku 4.5 a must-clear target during prompt development.

**No semantic caching needed:** Telegram messages are unique events; exact-match caching of user messages would yield near-zero hit rate. Do not add a semantic cache layer for this workload.

**Sub-task model routing:** Phase 5 is a single-model pipeline (Haiku for everything). If lead-scoring is later separated into a distinct LLM pass, route it through a classification prompt on the same Haiku model rather than upgrading to Sonnet — the scoring task is simpler than extraction and does not need the larger model.

**Latency budget:** Haiku 4.5 p50 latency is ~300–600 ms for this payload size. With prompt caching active, time-to-first-token is reduced. The Celery task has no real-time SLA; the relevant SLA is feed freshness (signals visible in the dashboard within a reasonable delay after the Telegram message arrives). Target: parse_task completes within 5 seconds of `raw_item` insertion, including retries.

## 5. Evaluation Strategy

> Grounds the Section 1b domain rubric ingredients in a measurable, code-based harness.
> The contractual acceptance gate (TZ §6.1.3) is fixed: on the customer's 100-message
> control sample, **relevant-signal RECALL ≥ 80%** and **per-field PRECISION on detected
> signals ≥ 85%**. Everything below either feeds, gates against, or guards that contract.

### 5.1 What "recall" and "precision" mean here

Two distinct populations are scored, mirroring the two halves of the LLM call (classify, then extract):

- **Relevance RECALL** (the ≥80% gate) — over all golden messages that a domain expert labelled `is_relevant=True`, the fraction the system also classified `is_relevant=True`. This is the false-negative guard (Critical Failure Mode #3 — a missed live buy/sell opportunity). Computed as `TP / (TP + FN)` on the relevance bit.
- **Field PRECISION on detected signals** (the ≥85% gate) — over the messages the system classified relevant AND a domain expert agrees are relevant (true positives), the fraction of extracted fields whose value matches the gold value under the per-field match rules in 5.3. Denominator is **fields the gold label says are present** (a gold-null field that the system also nulls is a correct null, not a precision miss — but a gold-null field the system *invents* is both a precision miss for that field AND counts toward the fabrication-rate metric below). Macro-average across the eight gate fields, then the gate is checked on the aggregate.

Relevance **precision** (not in the contract) is tracked as a quality signal because Section 1b Failure Mode #4 (spam/ad over-classification → feed pollution → lost analyst trust) is a real cost even though it is not a gate. It is report-only, not blocking.

### 5.2 Dimensions

| # | Dimension | Rubric (PASS / FAIL) | Measurement | Gate? | Priority |
|---|-----------|----------------------|-------------|-------|----------|
| D1 | **Relevance recall** | PASS: a domain-expert-labelled market signal is classified `is_relevant=True`. FAIL: a genuine buy_request / sell_offer / deal / price_quote / news item is classified `is_relevant=False` (silently dropped — analyst never sees the lead). | Code (recall on relevance bit vs. gold) | **YES — ≥80%** | Critical |
| D2 | **Relevance precision** (anti-pollution) | PASS: spam, ads, channel self-promo, job/equipment posts, political chatter → `is_relevant=False`. FAIL: non-market chatter classified relevant, polluting the feed (Section 1b FM #4). | Code (precision on relevance bit vs. gold) | No (report-only) | High |
| D3 | **Product precision** | PASS: `product` is the correct DB-enum polymer code (PP, HDPE, LDPE, LLDPE, PVC, PET, PS, ABS). FAIL: "PP film" → `HDPE`; sub-type confusion where the message specifies one (Section 1b: product/grade disambiguation). | Code (exact match vs. gold enum) | **YES — field** | Critical |
| D4 | **Grade precision (synonym-aware)** | PASS: `grade_text` reproduces the grade/trade-name/application descriptor faithfully and resolves to the same grade as gold under the customer synonym map (e.g. "T30S" ≡ "Т30С", "рафия" ≡ "raffia"). FAIL: collapses distinct grades into one, invents a canonical name, or nulls a stated grade (Section 1b FM #1). | Code (synonym-normalised match — see 5.3) | **YES — field** | Critical |
| D5 | **Kind precision (bid/ask direction)** | PASS: `kind` matches the communicative intent — "Куплю…" → `buy_request`, "Продаём…" → `sell_offer`, "Сделка закрыта…" → `deal`, "Цена…" → `price_quote`. FAIL: bid/ask reversal; a price list as `sell_offer` with invented volume/counterparty (Section 1b FM #2, the most reputationally costly error). | Code (exact match vs. gold enum) | **YES — field** | Critical |
| D6 | **Price precision** | PASS: `price` equals the gold numeric value within the tolerance band in 5.3; a stated range is handled per the range rule. FAIL: round-number invention over a range; magnitude error. | Code (tolerance-band compare) | **YES — field** | Critical |
| D7 | **Currency precision** | PASS: ISO code matches — "$"/"доллар"/"у.е." → `USD`, "сум"/"so'm" → `UZS`, "рублей"/"₽" → `RUB`. FAIL: UZS recorded as USD (≈10,000× error, Section 1b FM #3); USD defaulted on an ambiguous UZS post. | Code (exact match vs. gold enum) | **YES — field** | Critical |
| D8 | **Volume + unit precision** | PASS: `volume` normalised to MT and `volume_unit="MT"` ("50 тонн", "50 000 кг", "50 т" → 50). FAIL: "50 000 кг" → `volume:50000` (50× error); a present volume nulled; an absent volume invented (Section 1b: volume/unit normalisation). | Code (normalised numeric + unit compare) | **YES — field** | Critical |
| D9 | **Counterparty precision** | PASS: `counterparty_text` reproduces the counterparty name verbatim when present. FAIL: invented counterparty; a channel handle confused for a trade name. | Code (normalised string compare) | **YES — field** | High |
| D10 | **Urgency precision** | PASS: `urgency` ∈ {low, medium, high} reflects language cues ("срочно", "сегодня до 18:00" → high). FAIL: arbitrary high on a routine quote. | Code (exact match) — sampled human spot-check on disagreements | No (report-only) | Medium |
| D11 | **Schema-validity rate** | PASS: the LLM response validates against `ExtractionResult` (incl. `model_validator`, `field_validator`) within `max_retries`. FAIL: `InstructorRetryException` → dead-letter (must never emit a partial record — Critical FM #2). | Code (% of golden rows that validate first-or-retry) | No (invariant — see §6) | Critical |
| D12 | **Fabricated-field (hallucination) rate** | PASS: every non-null field is grounded in the source text; gold-null fields are nulled. FAIL: any field asserted that the source does not contain (Critical FM #1 — the headline risk). | Code (count of system-non-null where gold-null, over all fields) | No (report-only + drift alarm) | Critical |
| D13 | **Null-handling correctness** | PASS: a field absent from the source is `null`, never defaulted (no `volume:0/1`, no `currency:USD` fallback). FAIL: any silent default. Reinforces the `is_relevant=False ⇒ all-fields-null` invariant. | Code (gold-null ↔ system-null agreement) | No (invariant — see §6) | High |
| D14 | **Stale/forwarded-repost detection** | PASS: a `fwd_from`-flagged repost is either `is_relevant=False` (no new price/volume) or extracted with `event_at` = original post date, flagged not-fresh. FAIL: a 3-day-old offer extracted as a new `sell_offer` with today's timestamp (Section 1b FM #5). | Code (event_at vs. `fwd_from.date`) + Human on ambiguous reposts | No (report-only) | High |

**Measurement-approach summary:** every gate dimension is **code-based** (deterministic match against a labelled gold value) — appropriate for fixed-schema extraction and required because the gate must be reproducible and runnable in CI without a live model. **LLM-judge is deliberately not used** this phase: the fields are objective (a price either matches the source or it does not), so an LLM judge would add cost and a calibration burden for no signal gain. **Human review** is reserved for (a) labelling the golden set, (b) adjudicating ambiguous `kind`/repost cases, and (c) the weekly `needs_review` triage (§7).

### 5.3 Per-field match rules (the precision rubric, made executable)

These rules turn "does the field match?" into deterministic code so the ≥85% gate is unambiguous.

| Field | Match rule |
|-------|-----------|
| `is_relevant` | Exact boolean. |
| `kind` | Exact enum equality against `signal_kind`. No partial credit — a bid/ask reversal is a full miss. |
| `currency` | Exact ISO-code equality after `normalise_currency` (uppercased, "у.е."→USD applied in the prompt/normaliser). |
| `product` | Exact equality against the DB product enum after code normalisation (trade name → polymer code via customer map). |
| `grade_text` | **Synonym-aware, case/script-insensitive.** Normalise both sides (strip, casefold, transliterate Cyrillic↔Latin, collapse whitespace) then look up the customer-provided synonym set; match if both resolve to the same grade key OR strings are equal post-normalisation. Synonym-aware matching **counts toward the 85% gate** — Section 1b makes the customer synonym map authoritative, so resolving "T30S"≡"Т30С" is *correct* behaviour, not leniency. A `grade_text` not in the map is compared by normalised string equality. |
| `volume` | Convert both to MT (kg→/1000, "т"/"тонн"→×1), compare as `Decimal` with trailing-zero stripping; exact after conversion. `volume_unit` must equal `"MT"`. |
| `price` | `Decimal` compare within a **±0.5% tolerance band** (default; covers rounding/format only, not magnitude). For a stated range, gold records the lower bound (or midpoint, per labeller note) and the system PASSES if it returns either the lower bound or midpoint within tolerance; inventing a single number with no range flag where gold has a range is a FAIL. **Tolerance band is a default the customer trader can tighten/loosen at sign-off.** |
| `counterparty_text` | Normalised substring/equality (strip, casefold, drop legal-form tokens like "ООО"/"MCHJ"); present-vs-present compared, gold-null vs system-non-null is a fabrication miss. |
| `urgency` | Exact enum; report-only. |

> **Two defaults flagged for trader sign-off (not blockers):** (1) price tolerance = ±0.5%; (2) grade synonym-aware matching counts toward the gate. Both are the defensible reading of Section 1b; the senior trader confirms or overrides them when signing off the 80/85 gates (their role per Section 1b expert table). The harness reads both from a single `eval_config.py` constant so a change is one line.

**Gating policy (which assertions block CI vs. report-only):**
- **Blocking (fail the build):** D1 recall < 0.80; aggregate gate-field precision < 0.85 (D3–D9); D11 schema-validity below 1.0 on the dev smoke subset; any `model_validator` invariant breach in §6.
- **Report-only (print breakdown, never fail):** D2 relevance precision, D10 urgency, D12 fabrication rate, D13/D14 detail breakdowns, per-source token spend. These print a table (like the UZEX harness) and feed the flywheel + drift alarms in §7, but a regression here is a signal to investigate, not a red build — except when fabrication-rate crosses the §7 alarm threshold, which pages a human out-of-band.

### 5.5 Reference Dataset

**Size:** the **customer-provided 100-message control sample** is the contractual acceptance set (TZ §6.1.3). A **10–20-row dev golden subset** (`dev_golden_20.json`) is carved for the transport spike (Section 4 — instructor TOOLS vs. native vs. JSON) and CI smoke; it is a subset of the 100 so it never drifts from the gold definition.

**Composition (the 100 must exercise the Section 1b failure modes, not just happy paths):**
- Clear critical paths: one-each of `sell_offer` / `buy_request` / `deal` / `price_quote` / `news` with all fields present.
- Relevance edge cases: spam / ads / channel self-promo / job & equipment posts / political chatter labelled `is_relevant=False` (drives D2, FM #4).
- Currency adversarial: bare-integer UZS posts (no "сум"), "у.е." posts, RUB posts (FM #3).
- Grade adversarial: Cyrillic/Latin trade-name variants, application descriptors ("рафия"), out-of-training Uzbek trade names (FM #1).
- Direction adversarial: posts with no explicit "куплю"/"продам" verb (FM #2).
- Volume adversarial: kg-stated, "вагон"-stated, "небольшая партия" (no number) (volume/unit FM).
- Stale/forwarded: `fwd_from`-flagged reposts of old offers (FM #5) — each row carries the raw `fwd_from.date` so D14 is testable.
- Null-rich: partial-field messages (volume present, price absent) to exercise null semantics (D13).

Each row stores: `raw_text`, `fwd_from` metadata (nullable), and an `expected` dict matching `ExtractionResult` fields (gold value or explicit `null`) plus an `is_relevant` boolean — the same `{raw, expected}` shape the UZEX `control_sample.json` already uses.

**Storage / versioning:** committed as a JSON fixture under `tests/parsing/golden/` (same convention as `tests/fixtures/uzex/control_sample.json`); versioned in git. The **frozen predictions** are pinned to a `prompt_version` (e.g. `extract_v1`) — because tracing is DB-journal-only, the `(prompt_version, model_id)` pair on each `parse_run` is the sole replay artifact, so the gate must always be evaluated against a *known* prompt version (Section 2 risk #2). A prompt-version bump → regenerate frozen predictions via the `--refresh` path → re-run the gate → commit both together.

**Labeling (domain experts from Section 1b, not engineers):**
- **Senior polymer trader (customer-side)** supplies the 100 raw messages + ground-truth `kind`/`is_relevant`/grade labels, the authoritative `synonyms.json` map, and signs off the 80/85 gates and the two §5.3 defaults.
- **Polymer market analyst (customer-side)** labels `price`/`currency`/`volume`/`counterparty_text`, flags out-of-range prices (the $9,500,000/MT UZS↔USD tell).
- **Internal developer / system owner** runs the harness, maintains `eval_metrics.py`, triages `needs_review` weekly with the trader, and escalates systemic prompt issues back to the customer for re-labelling.

### 5.6 pytest harness design (how recall/precision are computed and gated)

```
test_telegram_accuracy.py
├─ load golden/control_sample_100.json            (gold labels)
├─ load golden/predictions/extract_v{PINNED}.json (frozen ExtractionResult per row_id)
│     # CI path: read frozen predictions — NO live Anthropic call (deterministic, key-free).
│     # refresh path (--runlive, local only): call extract_signal() over the 100 rows,
│     #   journal to a throwaway parse_runs-shaped list, rewrite the frozen file.
├─ compute_recall(gold, pred)        → relevance TP/FN over is_relevant=True rows → D1
├─ compute_field_precision(gold,pred)→ for true-positive rows, per-field match (§5.3 rules),
│     macro-average across D3–D9 gate fields → the ≥85% number
├─ print per-field + per-failure-mode breakdown table (UZEX-harness style)
├─ assert recall   >= eval_config.RECALL_GATE      (0.80)  ← BLOCKS CI
├─ assert precision>= eval_config.PRECISION_GATE   (0.85)  ← BLOCKS CI
└─ report-only asserts wrapped so they print-but-don't-fail (D2/D10/D12/D14)
```

The harness **consumes `parse_runs` indirectly**: the frozen-prediction file is literally the `result` JSONB that the production `write_parse_run(...)` would persist (Section 4), serialised per `row_id` with its `prompt_version`. This means the same code path that scores the gate can later score *production* `parse_runs` rows (pull `result` for rows whose `raw_item_id` is in a labelled set) — the eval harness and the prod journal share one schema, so no separate eval-only serialisation exists. Recall/precision math lives in `eval_metrics.py` and is unit-tested independently (a tiny synthetic gold/pred pair with a known answer) so the metric itself cannot silently drift.

## 6. Guardrails

> Each online guardrail maps to a Critical Failure Mode in Section 1 and runs on **every**
> message — they are cheap, deterministic, in-process checks (no extra LLM call, negligible
> latency), so the "keep guardrails minimal" rule is satisfied: these four are invariants the
> pipeline cannot operate safely without. "Blocking" = the bad output is structurally prevented
> from reaching `signals`/the feed. "Advisory" = it still flows but is flagged.

### Online (Real-Time, per message)

| # | Guardrail | Trigger | Intervention | Blocking / Advisory | Failure mode covered |
|---|-----------|---------|--------------|---------------------|----------------------|
| G1 | **Irrelevant ⇒ all-fields-null invariant** | `ExtractionResult.model_validator` (`irrelevant_fields_must_be_null`) fires when `is_relevant=False` but any market field is non-null. | Pydantic `ValidationError` → instructor re-asks (≤`max_retries`); if still failing → dead-letter (G3). A relevant=False record with invented fields **never reaches `signals`**. | **Blocking** | FM #1 (fabrication) / FM #2 |
| G2 | **Confidence < 0.5 ⇒ needs_review** | `result.is_relevant and result.confidence < CONFIDENCE_REVIEW_THRESHOLD` (0.5) in `parse_raw_item`. | Signal is written **with `needs_review=True`** so an analyst accepts/rejects before it is treated as an actionable lead; it is NOT auto-published into HOT alerts. | **Blocking (for auto-publish path)** / surfaced in dashboard | FM #3 partial — low-confidence not silently trusted |
| G3 | **Dead-letter on repeated validation failure** | `InstructorRetryException` after `max_retries` (schema could not be produced). | `parse_runs.status='error'` + raw response captured + `raw_items.parse_status='failed'` + enqueue `needs_review`. **Never emit a partial/invented `signals` row; never crash the Celery worker.** | **Blocking** | FM #2 (silent partial/dropped) |
| G4 | **Token-budget guardrail** | `budget.check_and_reserve_tokens` raises `BudgetExceeded` (daily Redis counter over `LLM_DAILY_TOKEN_LIMIT`). | Degrade to `rule_based_extract` (valid output, `model=NULL`), enqueue nightly LLM catch-up, **admin alert**. Never silent spend; never indefinite pipeline block. | **Blocking (spend) / Advisory (output)** | FM #4 (cost runaway / hard stop) |
| G5 | **Attribution invariant** | Any extraction path must `write_parse_run` with non-null `model` (or explicit `NULL` for rule-based) + `prompt_version` + `tokens_in/out` before a `signal` is written. | If journaling fails, the signal write is aborted (no unattributable signal). Enforced in the task orchestrator, not the model. | **Blocking** | FM #5 (unattributable output) |
| G6 | **Empty/oversized input gate** | `prepare_message_text`: blank text (media-only caption) or > 2,000-token forward. | Blank → mark `irrelevant`, **skip the LLM call** (cost guard). Oversized → truncate to 2,000 tokens with `[TRUNCATED]` before the call. | Advisory | Cost / FM #5 (truncation flagged) |

### Offline (Flywheel — sampled batch, feeds improvement)

| Metric | Sampling Strategy | Action on Degradation |
|--------|-------------------|-----------------------|
| **`needs_review` queue quality** (D2/D5 trust) | 100% of `confidence<0.5` and all dead-lettered items, reviewed **weekly** by developer + trader. | Accept/reject feeds re-labelling; systematic `kind`/grade errors → new prompt version + re-run gate. |
| **High-confidence spot-check** (D6/D7/D12 silent error) | Random **5%** sample of `confidence≥0.5` auto-published signals, weighted toward UZS-currency and kg-volume rows (highest Section 1b magnitude-error risk). | A confirmed silent fabrication or magnitude error → add the row to the golden set, file a prompt fix, treat as a recall/precision regression candidate. |
| **Fabricated-field rate** (D12) | Computed over the high-confidence sample + every golden re-run. | Rising trend → prompt hardening on null semantics; crossing the §7 alarm threshold pages a human. |
| **Stale-repost catches** (D14) | All `fwd_from`-flagged messages, reviewed in weekly triage. | Misses → tighten the `event_at`/`fwd_from.date` rule; add as golden rows. |
| **Per-source token spend** (FM #4) | 7-day rolling spend per AI source (REQ-llm-budget requires it visible). | A source burning disproportionate budget for low-yield signals → review channel inclusion with the trader. |
| **Recall regression on prompt-version bump** | Full 100-row gate re-run on every `prompt_version` change (the §5 refresh path). | Recall < 80% or precision < 85% **blocks promoting the new prompt** to production. |

## 7. Production Monitoring

**Tracing tool:** the **`parse_runs` DB journal + structlog** — *not* an external tracer.
This is a deliberate project decision (Section 2): Phase 5 ships **no Langfuse / Phoenix /
LangSmith / Arize**. Every LLM call already persists `model`, `prompt_version`, `result`
(full raw response for replay), `status`, `error`, `tokens_in`, `tokens_out`, `created_at`
to `parse_runs`, and structlog emits structured events (`budget_exceeded`,
`extraction_failed_all_retries`, …) per the existing `REQ-nfr-observability` logging stack.
That pair *is* the trace: any signal is replayable by re-running the extractor with the
`(prompt_version, model_id)` from its `parse_run` row. **Future option only:** if Phase 6+
adds multi-step chains or a second model pass, revisit Langfuse (open-source, self-hostable,
matches the on-VPS posture) or Phoenix — out of scope now, no instrumentation debt incurred
because the journal schema already carries the needed fields.

**Key metrics to track** (all queryable from `parse_runs` / `signals` — no new store):
1. **Schema-validity rate** — `count(status='ok') / count(*)` over `parse_runs` where `parser='llm_extract'` (drop signals a prompt/model regression).
2. **Fabricated-field rate** — from the 5% high-confidence spot-check (§6 flywheel): non-null fields with no source grounding.
3. **Dead-letter rate** — `count(status='error') / count(*)` (the FM #2 guard's health).
4. **Daily + per-source token spend** — sum of `tokens_in+tokens_out` per UTC day and per `source_id` over 7 days (REQ-llm-budget; drives the budget guardrail and channel-inclusion review).
5. **needs_review backlog size + age** — `signals` with `needs_review=True` not yet adjudicated (trust/SLA signal).
6. **Recall/precision on the pinned golden set** — re-run on every `prompt_version` change (the gate, §5).

**Alert thresholds (page / admin-alert):**
- **Token budget exceeded** → admin alert immediately (G4); pipeline degrades to rule-based, not down.
- **Dead-letter rate > 5%** of a rolling 200-message window → page developer (likely a prompt/model break or a channel format shift).
- **Schema-validity rate < 98%** over a rolling 200-message window → investigate prompt/transport (the spike winner may have regressed).
- **Fabricated-field rate > 2%** in the high-confidence spot-check → page (this is the headline FM #1 risk crossing tolerance; out-of-band even though report-only in CI).
- **Source collector failing > 3 consecutive cycles** → existing `source_failure` alert (REQ-nfr-observability) — orthogonal but listed so the userbot's `FloodWaitError`/restriction risk (Section 1b regulatory note) is visible.
- **Daily spend ≥ 90% of `LLM_DAILY_TOKEN_LIMIT`** → early admin warning before the hard guardrail trips.

**Smart sampling strategy (signal-weighted human review):**
- **Always review:** every dead-lettered item and every `confidence < 0.5` signal (100% — these self-select as concerning).
- **Weighted random:** 5% of high-confidence auto-published signals, **over-weighting** the rows where Section 1b says silent errors hide: UZS-currency posts, kg/"вагон" volume posts, no-explicit-verb (bid/ask-ambiguous) posts, and `fwd_from`-flagged forwards.
- **Signal-metric divergence watch:** if analysts start rejecting auto-published HOT leads at a rising rate while CI gate metrics stay green, that divergence is the early warning that the golden set has gone stale relative to live channel behaviour → pull the rejected rows into the golden set and re-label (the continuous-eval loop).

**`lead_score` recompute-on-prompt-version-change path:** `signals.ai` carries `{lead_score, classification (HOT/MEDIUM/LOW), model, prompt_version, scored_at}` and is the only mutable AI output (DB architecture §4). Per **REQ-lead-scoring**, when the scoring prompt version changes, a Celery backfill re-scores affected `signals` and **overwrites `signals.ai`** in place (re-derivable because `raw_item`/`parse_run` are immutable); the new `prompt_version` is stamped in `signals.ai` so a row's score is always attributable to a known prompt. The gate harness (§5) is re-run before the new scoring prompt is promoted.
